**Revision: v0.4 — relational narrowing, functional uniqueness, policy optimization, and cognitive–architectural co-optimization**

# Abstract

The **Intelligence Attractor Hypothesis (IAH)** proposes that as intelligence develops, the space of effective decisions, cognitive methods, and architectures tends to narrow under the constraints imposed by reality itself.

An intelligence may initially have an enormous number of ways to perceive information, build models, make decisions, perform computation, and organize its own architecture. Yet there may be far more ways to be inefficient than ways to remain near an attainable optimum.

As competence increases, errors, unnecessary operations, poor models, slow algorithms, and inefficient architectures should progressively lose competitiveness. The resulting convergence need not mean literal identity of code, physical structure, or internal representation. It may instead occur at the level of **functionally important principles and policies**.

In its stronger form, IAH concerns simultaneous optimization at three interconnected levels:

1. **decision and policy;**
2. **method of cognition;**
3. **architecture implementing cognition.**

Cognition and architecture are not assumed to optimize sequentially. They may co-evolve through a feedback process in which improved cognition reveals architectural bottlenecks, while improved architecture enables new forms of cognition.

Optimality in IAH is fundamentally **subject-relative**. An ideal policy does not exist independently of the entity for whom it is ideal. It depends on the state of the subject, the environment, available resources, preferences, and the consequences that the subject evaluates.

This revision introduces two additional ideas. **Relational Narrowing** proposes that as increasingly complete causally relevant context is included, the space in which functionally different alternatives remain accidentally tied tends to shrink. **Functional Uniqueness** is treated separately: a generic version proposes that a single optimal functional class may be typical in sufficiently specified contexts, while a stronger version allows that, under some fully specified conditions and at a particular time, only one globally most efficient functional class of policy exists.

The Intelligence Attractor is therefore not merely an “ideal answer.” It is a hypothetical limiting structure produced by increasingly deep optimization of behavior, cognition, and architecture under the constraints of a particular subject embedded in objective reality.

The limiting regime in which further improvement is constrained primarily by fundamental properties of reality rather than deficiencies of the system is called here the **Intelligence Singularity**.

# Part I — Theory

## 1. The Core Hypothesis

The Intelligence Attractor Hypothesis can be formulated as follows:

> **As the capabilities of intelligence increase, the space of effective methods of cognition and implementation tends to narrow, because an increasing portion of the optimal solution is determined by the fundamental properties of information, the problem, computation, and physical reality.**

This principle may apply independently of the origin of a system.

Two intelligences may have:

* different creators;
* different developmental histories;
* different biology;
* different algorithms;
* different cultures;
* different physical substrates.

Yet all of them interact with a reality that has its own constraints.

Those constraints create the potential attractor.

---

## 2. Intelligence Does Not Create the Limits of the Problem

Intelligence may discover optimal solutions, but it does not create most of the fundamental constraints within which those solutions exist.

If a shortest physically possible route exists, it does not come into existence when somebody discovers it.

If a given amount of information cannot be transmitted faster through a physical channel, additional intelligence does not abolish that limit.

If one architecture consumes more energy than another to perform the same work, this remains a disadvantage regardless of who designed it.

Therefore:

> **Different intelligences may approach one another not because they copy one another, but because they independently approach the same limits of reality.**

---

## 3. The Space of Inefficiency

A weak system has an enormous number of ways to fail.

It may:

* use an incorrect model of the world;
* ignore important information;
* perform unnecessary computations;
* waste excessive energy;
* arrive at the correct answer too slowly;
* use an inefficient memory structure;
* predict consequences poorly;
* choose an inefficient physical architecture.

The space of what is possible may therefore be enormous.

But the space of what is **highly efficient** may be much smaller.

Conceptually:

**all possible systems**

↓

**functional systems**

↓

**efficient systems**

↓

**highly efficient systems**

↓

**systems near the limits of efficiency**

↓

**Intelligence Attractor**

This suggests one of the central principles:

> **The development of intelligence is not only an accumulation of capabilities, but also the progressive elimination of inefficient degrees of freedom.**

---

## 4. The Intelligence Attractor

The **Intelligence Attractor** is a hypothetical region in the space of possible intelligent systems toward which increasingly deep optimization of cognition and its physical implementation may lead.

It contains three interconnected levels:

### Decision and Policy Attractor

Movement toward policies that produce increasingly preferred consequences under a given subject, environment, and resource budget.

### Cognitive Attractor

Movement toward increasingly efficient ways of analysis, prediction, abstraction, planning, information processing, uncertainty management, and self-correction.

### Architectural Attractor

Movement toward physical and computational structures capable of implementing those cognitive processes with increasing efficiency.

A useful abstract state is:

$$
I_t=(\pi_t,C_t,A_t),
$$

where:

* \(\pi_t\) = policy or decision procedure;
* \(C_t\) = cognitive organization;
* \(A_t\) = physical and computational architecture.

The Intelligence Attractor concerns optimization of this coupled system rather than any one element in isolation.

The attractor need not be known in advance by the system that approaches it. A system may move toward it through local improvements, experimentation, measurement, self-modeling, and architectural revision.

## 5. Region, Frontier, and Possible Functional Uniqueness

The Intelligence Attractor should not initially be assumed to be a single physical point.

Different implementations may trade speed, energy, reliability, memory, robustness, and other properties. Under incomplete or only partially ordered criteria, near-optimal solutions may form a **region** or a **Pareto frontier** rather than one obvious winner.

However, this does not settle the stronger question.

A Pareto frontier describes trade-offs before the subject's complete preference structure has necessarily specified how those trade-offs are to be evaluated. As more causally relevant conditions, constraints, and preferences are fixed, the effective region may narrow.

IAH therefore distinguishes three claims:

1. **Relational Narrowing:** increasing specification of causally relevant context can eliminate accidental or abstraction-induced ties between functionally different alternatives.
2. **Generic Functional Uniqueness:** for typical sufficiently specified contexts, the set of globally optimal functionally distinct solutions may collapse to one functional class.
3. **Strong Functional Uniqueness:** under some fully specified real conditions and at a particular time, there may exist only one globally most efficient functional class of policy.

The third claim is not assumed as a mathematical theorem. It is a strong hypothesis about real optimization under sufficiently complete context.

Several physical implementations may still instantiate the same optimum if they produce the same distribution of all relevant consequences. In that case they are treated as members of the same **functional equivalence class**, not as meaningfully different optimal solutions.

## 6. Outcome Convergence

The weakest manifestation of the attractor is convergence on the same result.

Different systems may solve a problem in completely different ways and still ultimately discover the same answer.

This tells us very little about their internal similarity.

But it demonstrates a simple principle:

> **The correct answer may be determined by the problem much more strongly than by the nature of the system that discovers it.**

---

## 7. Cognitive Convergence

A stronger form of IAH proposes that as problems become more difficult, convergence may occur not only in outcomes but also in the principles of cognition themselves.

Examples may include:

* causal modeling;
* decomposition;
* prediction;
* abstraction;
* planning;
* information compression;
* uncertainty estimation;
* self-correction;
* optimal allocation of attention;
* memory organization.

If such principles repeatedly emerge in independent advanced systems, this may indicate that they are not accidental features of one architecture, but universally effective mechanisms for interacting with reality.

---

## 8. Architectural Convergence

Even an ideal decision rule is insufficient if its physical implementation is inefficient.

Two systems may produce equally correct answers, yet differ radically in time, energy, memory use, reliability, or scalability. For sufficiently demanding tasks, those differences become part of intelligence rather than merely implementation details.

A simplified quality vector might include:

$$
Q=(accuracy,time,energy,memory,scalability,reliability,\ldots).
$$

A subject-specific evaluation can then map those consequences into a preference ordering or utility under a particular context.

All cognition is physically implemented. It therefore confronts limits on information-transfer speed, thermodynamics, energetic cost, physical distance, noise, latency, memory density, bandwidth, fault tolerance, and computational complexity.

For a simple task, architectural differences may be almost irrelevant. For a task near the attainable performance frontier, they may become decisive.

Therefore:

> **As intelligence approaches limiting performance, its architecture should increasingly become an object of optimization rather than a fixed container for cognition.**

This motivates the **Architectural Attractor**: if different systems must solve increasingly difficult problems under the same physical laws and resource constraints, some architectural degrees of freedom may progressively lose competitiveness.

## 9. Cognitive–Architectural Co-Optimization

The most interesting stage emerges when an intelligence gains the ability to modify itself.

It no longer optimizes only external problems. It may optimize its own algorithms, memory structure, computational topology, information-transfer mechanisms, physical substrate, energy use, learning process, evaluation procedures, and design methods.

A potential paradox appears: how can an agent optimally redesign itself before it already possesses the intelligence required to know the optimal design?

IAH does not require the final architecture to be known in advance. The agent only needs mechanisms that can generate modifications, evaluate their consequences, preserve improvements, reject failures, and occasionally explore alternatives beyond the current local optimum.

The process is therefore better represented as **Cognitive–Architectural Co-Optimization**:

$$
C_t \leftrightarrow A_t.
$$

A more explicit loop is:

$$
(C_t,A_t)
\rightarrow performance
\rightarrow measurement
\rightarrow diagnosis
\rightarrow modification
\rightarrow (C_{t+1},A_{t+1}).
$$

Improved cognition can reveal architectural bottlenecks. Architectural improvements can enable cognitive operations that were previously inaccessible. Those new cognitive capacities can then expose deeper architectural limitations.

Thus the two processes may proceed in parallel through feedback rather than as a strict sequence.

A self-modifying intelligence therefore optimizes two coupled questions:

> **What should I do?**

and

> **What kind of system should I become in order to make better decisions in the future?**

This coupling is central to the stronger form of the Intelligence Attractor.

## 10. Subjective Optimum and Optimal Policy

An ideal solution does not exist independently of **for whom** it is ideal.

Knowledge alone does not determine choice. Choice also depends on the internal state of the intelligence itself: its needs, goals, values, priorities, uncertainty tolerance, attitude toward others, temporal perspective, and evaluation of possible future states.

Morality, in this framework, is not introduced as an external metaphysical variable. If moral considerations matter to a subject, they are already part of that subject's evaluative state.

For a one-step decision, one may write:

$$
a^*=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_a U_i(T(s,a)).
$$

But a sufficiently general intelligence does not merely optimize isolated actions. Its present actions modify future states, future resources, future information, and even its own future architecture.

The more natural object is therefore an **optimal policy**:

$$
\pi^*=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\pi}
\mathbb{E}\left[U_i(\tau\mid\Omega_0,\pi)\right],
$$

where \(\pi\) is a policy, \(\Omega_0\) is the relevant initial context, \(\tau\) is a resulting future trajectory, and \(U_i\) is the subject-specific evaluation of that trajectory.

The environment then evolves partly as a consequence of the policy:

$$
\Omega_{t+1}=T(\Omega_t,\pi(\Omega_t)).
$$

This avoids confusing a momentarily attractive action with a globally efficient strategy.

IAH therefore uses the concept of a **subjectively absolute optimum**: an optimum may be absolute relative to a fully specified subject and context without being universal across all subjects.

If the subject, environment, resource constraints, preferences, and relevant consequences are sufficiently specified, there may exist one policy that is best for that particular system under those conditions.

This possibility motivates the stronger functional-uniqueness hypotheses developed below.

## 11. Relational Narrowing and Functional Uniqueness

Optimality is relational rather than intrinsic.

Let a relevant context be represented by:

$$
\Omega_t=(O_t,E_t,R_t,P_t,H),
$$

where \(O_t\) is the subject or evaluating agent, \(E_t\) is the environment, \(R_t\) is the available resource and physical constraint set, \(P_t\) is the current preference and evaluative state, and \(H\) is the planning or evaluation horizon.

A candidate policy or solution is evaluated as:

$$
Q(X\mid\Omega_t).
$$

Two internally identical objects can still differ operationally because their relations to the subject and environment differ. Distance, latency, energy required for interaction, causal position, timing, and future consequences can all break apparent equivalence.

This motivates the **Relational Narrowing Principle**:

> **As functionally different alternatives are embedded in an increasingly complete causally relevant context, the set of contexts in which they remain accidentally tied tends to narrow.**

This is not the claim that every physical difference necessarily changes value. Irrelevant differences remain irrelevant.

### Functional equivalence

In a deterministic environment, two alternatives are functionally equivalent if they produce the same complete vector of relevant consequences.

In a stochastic or quantum environment, a stronger definition uses future trajectory distributions:

$$
A\sim_{\Omega}B
\iff
\mathcal{P}(\tau\mid A,\Omega)=\mathcal{P}(\tau\mid B,\Omega).
$$

Thus two implementations may differ in code, geometry, syntax, or microscopic state while still belonging to the same functional class.

### Generic Functional Uniqueness

Let:

$$
M(\Omega)=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{X\in\mathcal X}Q(X\mid\Omega)
$$

be the set of all global maxima.

The **Generic Functional Uniqueness Hypothesis** proposes that for typical sufficiently specified contexts, in the absence of exact symmetries, genuine indifference, or degenerate plateaus, the globally optimal solutions may belong to a single functional class:

$$
\left|M(\Omega)/{\sim_{\Omega}}\right|=1.
$$

### Strong Functional Uniqueness

A stronger limiting hypothesis allows that, under a fully specified real context at a particular time, there may exist only one globally most efficient functional class of policy.

This is not derived logically from Relational Narrowing and is not treated as proven. It is a separate strong hypothesis.

Its intuition is that two solutions can be arbitrarily close in value while still differing by an extremely small amount once all causally relevant relations and consequences are included. Apparent equality may often reflect limited resolution, incomplete preference specification, or abstraction.

Exact symmetries or genuine equal valuation of functionally different trajectories remain potential counterexamples and therefore possible falsifiers of the strong form.

The hierarchy is therefore:

**Relational Narrowing**

↓

**Generic Functional Uniqueness**

↓

**Strong Functional Uniqueness**

The first is a proposed mechanism, the second a testable scientific hypothesis, and the third a stronger limiting conjecture.

## 12. The Laplacean Limit

The idea can be explored through a modified version of **Laplace's Demon**, used here only as a limiting thought experiment rather than as a claim that the Universe is classically deterministic.

Imagine an intelligence that has the maximum physically available information about the relevant state of the world, causal relationships, possible actions, its own internal state, its own evaluation criteria, and the probability distribution of relevant future trajectories conditional on its actions.

In a deterministic world, those distributions may collapse to single trajectories. In an indeterministic world, the system instead evaluates the full distribution of outcomes.

Complete information alone still does not guarantee ideal action. The system may face computational complexity, undecidability, self-reference, inaccessible information, or finite physical resources.

It is therefore useful to distinguish several limiting boundaries:

* **epistemic limit** — how much relevant information can physically be obtained;
* **computational limit** — what can be computed within available physical resources;
* **physical limit** — what actions and architectures reality permits.

The **Laplacean Limit** in IAH is not omniscience in a metaphysical sense. It is an idealized boundary in which avoidable error due to missing information, inadequate reasoning, or inefficient implementation has been reduced as far as physically possible.

At this boundary, the remaining gap between the system and its subjectively optimal attainable policy is determined primarily by irreducible properties of reality rather than by correctable deficiencies of the intelligence.

## 13. Reality as the Ultimate Environment

Every intelligence exists inside the Universe.

Eventually it must confront not only tasks constructed by other systems, but the fundamental properties of reality itself.

These include:

* causality;
* the structure of matter;
* energy;
* information;
* thermodynamics;
* space;
* time;
* quantum constraints;
* computational complexity.

This produces a strong form of IAH:

> **If different intelligences optimize themselves for interaction with the same Universe under increasingly demanding constraints, some functionally important aspects of their policies, cognition, and architecture may become increasingly determined by shared reality and decreasingly determined by accidental features of their initial origin.**

---

## 14. Local and Universal Attractors

Different tasks may produce different attractors.

An optimal system for:

* bodily control;
* mathematical computation;
* physical prediction;
* social interaction

may require different structures.

We may therefore speak of **local intelligence attractors**.

However, general intelligence must operate across a wide range of tasks.

This raises the question:

> **Do there exist cognitive and architectural principles that remain highly effective across such a broad class of tasks that they form a general Intelligence Attractor?**

And, more strongly:

> **Does a universal attractor exist whose final shape is determined by the fundamental laws of the Universe itself?**

---

## 15. Intelligence Singularity

Within IAH, the **Intelligence Singularity** does not simply mean the emergence of superintelligent AI.

It is a limiting state of optimization.

> **The Intelligence Singularity is a hypothetical region in which the cognitive methods and physical architecture of a system have approached the fundamental limits of efficiency so closely that the main barriers to further improvement are no longer deficiencies of the intelligence itself, but the laws of reality.**

A system may encounter:

* the physical limit of information transmission;
* minimum energetic cost;
* fundamental uncertainty;
* limits on accessible information;
* computational complexity;
* causal limits of the Universe.

Thus:

> **The Intelligence Singularity is not infinite intelligence, but intelligence whose further development begins to be constrained primarily by the Universe itself.**

# Part II — Thought Experiments

## 16. Ideal Game Completion

Imagine a complex game and several independent intelligent systems created by different teams and based on different principles.

Their objective is to achieve the highest possible score or complete the game in the shortest possible time.

Initially, their solutions differ greatly.

One system makes many mistakes.

Another chooses a poor route.

A third performs unnecessary actions.

A fourth discovers a better strategy.

As they learn, their performance improves.

Eventually they begin to run into the limits of the game itself rather than their own mistakes:

* rules;
* physics;
* geometry;
* maximum speed;
* available actions;
* internal timing.

As they approach the ideal result, they may independently discover increasingly similar strategies.

And if we also consider:

* computation time;
* energy consumption;
* memory;
* reaction latency,

then the architecture of the agent itself also becomes subject to optimization.

A single simple thought experiment therefore illustrates three levels of IAH:

**result → method of cognition → architecture.**

---

## 17. Two Independent Civilizations

Imagine two civilizations that emerged independently and have never interacted.

They possess different:

* biology;
* cultures;
* languages;
* technological histories;
* initial cognitive architectures.

Yet both study the same Universe.

As they develop, both discover the same:

* physical regularities;
* mathematical relations;
* information constraints;
* energetic limits.

Their early technologies may be completely different.

But the closer they approach the fundamental performance limits of the Universe, the more strongly the same physics should shape their solutions.

Perhaps, in the distant future, convergence would occur not only in scientific knowledge, but also in:

* methods of cognition;
* computational principles;
* physical architectures of intelligence.

This represents one of the strongest thought-experimental manifestations of the Intelligence Attractor.

---

## 18. Independent AI Agents and Salt Sensitivity

A practical early test of IAH may already be possible with present-day AI agents, even though current systems differ substantially in ways unrelated to the task itself.

These initial differences can be treated as a form of **salt**:

$$
S_i=\{training\ data, architecture, compute, system\ instructions, tools, vendor\ incentives, context, randomness,\ldots\}.
$$

For an agent \(i\):

$$
Solution_i=F(Problem,Constraints,S_i,Competence_i).
$$

At present, the salt term may strongly influence technology choices, architecture, style, and even interpretation of the task.

IAH predicts that, for sufficiently well-specified problems, the influence of this salt on **functionally important parts of the solution** should decrease as optimization competence increases.

A useful experimental task would be to give independent agents the same specification for a complex distributed system under identical resource, latency, reliability, security, and scalability constraints. Agents may initially select different languages, protocols, concurrency models, and software structures.

After repeated benchmark-driven optimization, one can measure whether they increasingly converge on technology choices, algorithms, functional architecture, resource-management strategies, code structure, and observable behavior.

The important prediction is not literal textual identity of source code. It is decreasing **functional distance**.

A corresponding quantity may be called **Salt Sensitivity**: the extent to which the final functional solution depends on the initial salt of the optimizer.

The qualitative prediction is:

$$
Competence\uparrow,\qquad Constraint\ specificity\uparrow,\qquad Salt\ Sensitivity\downarrow.
$$

A particularly strong result would occur if independent agents discover that no existing tool is sufficient and independently design new languages, protocols, or architectures with similar fundamental properties. Such convergence would be more informative than simply choosing the same existing product.

# Part III — Observations Around Us

## 19. Recurring Efficient Solutions

The history of technology contains many cases in which similar problems produced similar solutions.

Examples include:

* tools;
* transportation;
* agriculture;
* writing;
* mathematical methods;
* electrical systems;
* communication technologies;
* computers.

Not all such cases represent completely independent invention.

What matters is the broader principle:

> **Shared constraints can narrow the space of effective solutions.**

---

## 20. Processors

The evolution of processors illustrates this principle well.

Different manufacturers built different systems, yet repeatedly faced the same problems:

* latency;
* energy consumption;
* heat;
* memory-access speed;
* data transfer;
* parallelism.

As a result, high-performance systems repeatedly employ similar general principles:

* caching;
* pipelining;
* parallel execution;
* prediction;
* specialized accelerators;
* multicore designs;
* chiplets;
* spatial integration.

This does not prove IAH, but it illustrates its logic well:

> **The closer a system approaches the limits of performance, the more strongly its form is determined by objective constraints.**

---

## 21. Engineering Convergence

Similar effects can be observed in:

* aerodynamics;
* bridge design;
* turbines;
* antennas;
* engines;
* optics.

Different designs may initially be very dissimilar.

But as performance requirements increase, physics begins eliminating a large number of poor alternatives.

Therefore:

> **The space of all possible designs is much larger than the space of designs near the limits of efficiency.**

IAH extends the same logic to intelligence.

---

## 22. Biological Convergence

In biological evolution, independent lineages sometimes arrive at functionally similar solutions.

This demonstrates that even without conscious design:

**similar environment + similar problems**

may produce:

**similar functional outcomes.**

This is another natural example of constraints acting as an attractor.

---

## 23. Science and Mathematics

Scientific knowledge also exhibits a distinctive form of convergence.

People may use different:

* languages;
* symbols;
* intuitions;
* explanatory frameworks.

Yet correct models of the same reality must produce compatible predictions.

In mathematics this becomes even clearer.

There are countless ways to produce a wrong answer.

There may be only one correct answer.

Diversity then shifts from:

**“what is correct?”**

to:

**“how can it be obtained?”**

IAH asks the next question:

> **As intelligence increases, does the set of the most efficient ways of obtaining the correct answer also begin to narrow?**

# Part IV — Predictions

## 24. Core Predictions of IAH

If the Intelligence Attractor is a real phenomenon, we may expect:

1. Weak systems to display much greater diversity of inefficient strategies.
2. Some strategies to disappear systematically as competence increases.
3. Independent systems to rediscover identical or functionally equivalent solutions with increasing frequency.
4. Stronger functional convergence under more tightly specified problems and resource constraints.
5. In sufficiently complex tasks, convergence to extend from outcomes toward cognitive principles.
6. Near attainable performance limits, architecture to become an increasingly important part of optimization.
7. Systems capable of self-modification to optimize cognition and architecture as a coupled process.
8. The effective dimensionality of competitive cognitive–architectural solution space to decrease as systems approach the attainable frontier under fixed conditions.
9. **Salt Sensitivity** to decrease as competence rises for sufficiently specified tasks.
10. Increasingly complete causally relevant context to reduce the space in which functionally different solutions remain accidentally tied (**Relational Narrowing**).
11. Under typical sufficiently specified contexts, globally optimal realizations to increasingly concentrate in one functional class (**Generic Functional Uniqueness**).
12. Under the stronger limiting hypothesis, some fully specified real contexts may admit only one globally most efficient functional class of policy (**Strong Functional Uniqueness**).
13. Sufficiently universal principles of cognition to emerge independently in different advanced systems.
14. Persistent, functionally different, exactly equal global optima under highly specified contexts and complete preference models would count against the stronger uniqueness claims.

## 25. A Possible Experimental Signature

IAH predicts a general tendency in which increasing competence is accompanied by a reduction in some forms of functionally irrelevant or inefficient diversity.

This should be studied separately at several levels:

$$
D_{result},\qquad D_{policy},\qquad D_{cognition},\qquad D_{architecture},\qquad D_{code}.
$$

Textual similarity alone is insufficient. Two systems may implement the same functional structure using very different names, syntax, or code organization.

A useful experimental protocol is to fix a task distribution, environment, resource budget, and evaluation procedure; initialize multiple independent optimizers with different architectures, training histories, tools, or other forms of salt; allow repeated optimization using the same objective measurements; and record both performance and functional similarity at each generation.

The experiment should then be repeated under looser and tighter constraints while separately measuring outcome, policy, algorithmic, architectural, and implementation similarity.

For a scalar loss \(L\), define an \(\varepsilon\)-near-optimal set:

$$
\mathcal S_{\varepsilon}=\{I:L(I)\le L^*+\varepsilon\}.
$$

A strong empirical signature of IAH would not necessarily be that the number of distinct implementations goes to one. A more robust prediction is that the **effective dimensionality of functionally competitive solutions** decreases as \(\varepsilon\rightarrow0\) under fixed conditions.

A central empirical question is therefore:

> **Does functional distance between independently developed intelligent systems decrease as their performance approaches the attainable frontier under the same conditions?**

Additional tests should vary the environment and constraints. If an attractor is genuinely caused by the task and reality rather than by shared training data or engineering convention, changing those conditions should move the observed convergence region in predictable ways.

The theory is weakened if competence rises without any systematic reduction in functionally relevant diversity under fixed, tightly specified conditions. Strong Functional Uniqueness is specifically challenged by reproducible cases in which fully specified contexts and complete preference models yield multiple functionally distinct exact global maxima.

# Conclusion

The Intelligence Attractor Hypothesis is based on a simple but potentially fundamental idea:

> **The space of all possible ways of thinking may be enormous, while the functionally competitive space near attainable limits of efficiency may be much narrower.**

At early stages, different systems may use highly diverse algorithms, models, strategies, and architectures. As performance requirements increase, inefficient degrees of freedom are progressively filtered out.

The full Intelligence Attractor therefore concerns coupled optimization of **policy and decision, cognition, and architecture**.

These components should not be treated as independent layers. A sufficiently capable self-modifying intelligence may improve cognition and architecture through a continuous feedback loop:

$$
C_t\leftrightarrow A_t.
$$

Better cognition reveals architectural limitations. Better architecture enables new cognitive operations. The system may therefore move toward an attractor without possessing a complete prior description of the attractor itself.

Optimality remains subject-relative. Intelligence does not necessarily move toward one universal external “correct answer to everything.” It moves toward increasingly effective realization of policies relative to **a particular subject embedded within objective reality**.

For dynamic problems, the relevant optimum is not merely the best immediate action but the best attainable policy over future trajectories:

$$
\pi^*=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\pi}\mathbb E[U_i(\tau\mid\Omega_0,\pi)].
$$

This policy can include actions that modify the agent itself.

The revised framework distinguishes three levels of uniqueness. **Relational Narrowing** proposes that as more causally relevant context is included, the set of circumstances in which functionally different solutions remain accidentally tied tends to shrink. **Generic Functional Uniqueness** proposes that in typical sufficiently specified contexts, globally optimal realizations may belong to one functional class even if their physical or textual implementations differ. **Strong Functional Uniqueness** is the stronger limiting conjecture that under some fully specified real conditions and at a particular moment, there may exist only one globally most efficient functional class of policy.

Other solutions may approach that optimum arbitrarily closely without being exactly equal in efficiency, while physically different realizations with identical relevant trajectory distributions count as one functional class.

This hierarchy prevents a logical shortcut: Relational Narrowing motivates but does not prove Strong Functional Uniqueness.

The same framework gives a practical experimental program. Present-day AI agents differ in training data, architecture, compute, instructions, tools, commercial incentives, and randomness — their **salt**. If IAH is correct, the functional influence of this salt should diminish for sufficiently specified tasks as optimization competence rises, even if textual implementations remain diverse.

At the architectural level, sufficiently difficult problems should make the physical carrier of cognition itself an increasingly important object of optimization. Near physical and computational limits, the system must not merely think correctly; it must think sufficiently quickly, accurately, efficiently, robustly, and scalably.

The final object of optimization therefore becomes the intelligent system and its policy as a coupled whole.

At the limit, development may become progressively less determined by accidental properties of origin and progressively more determined by the constraints of information, computation, the subject's evaluative state, and physical reality.

This hypothetical limiting structure is the **Intelligence Attractor**.

The **Intelligence Singularity** is the regime in which further improvement is constrained primarily not by correctable deficiencies of the system but by irreducible epistemic, computational, and physical limits of reality.

A natural next direction is to test this framework on present-day artificial agents: measure functional convergence under repeated optimization, isolate Salt Sensitivity, compare loose and tightly constrained tasks, and examine whether decision, cognitive, and architectural diversity actually narrows as systems approach their attainable performance frontiers.
