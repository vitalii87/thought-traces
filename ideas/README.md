# Intelligence Attractor Hypothesis — Research Map

The Intelligence Attractor research program studies whether optimization pressure reduces functionally relevant and origin-dependent diversity in intelligent systems, and whether such narrowing can extend from outcomes into policies, cognition, architecture, and other causally accessible implementation variables.

The program separates present-day empirical hypotheses from stronger theoretical and limiting conjectures.

## Claim Structure

- **E1 — Functional Narrowing:** empirical hypothesis.
- **E2 — Origin Attenuation:** empirical hypothesis.
- **D1 — Internal Convergence:** strong empirical extension.
- **R1 — Cross-Space Recurrence:** recursive extension.
- **U1 — Generic Functional Concentration:** domain-conditional strong hypothesis.
- **U2 — Strong-Limit Functional Uniqueness:** limiting conjecture.
- **O1 — Objective-Level Convergence:** not yet defined without an additional meta-objective or admissibility principle.

E1 and E2 are related but not logically nested. Failure of a stronger claim does not automatically falsify weaker claims, and a weak claim cannot be rescued by appealing to an inaccessible stronger level.

## Current Research Priority

The immediate PhD-oriented question is:

> Under what task, objective, constraint, and design-space conditions does functional diversity among independently optimized systems decrease as normalized regret approaches an attainable performance frontier?

The repository does not claim publication, peer review, institutional acceptance, or experimental validation.
