# Intelligence Attractor Hypothesis

**Vitalii Zhyliaiev**

The Intelligence Attractor Hypothesis (IAH) proposes that independently formed intelligent systems lose origin-contingent functional differences as they approach the same performance frontier under the same task, objective, context, and binding constraints.

## Read the Flagship Theory

### [The Intelligence Attractor Hypothesis — Canonical Statement](ideas/intelligence-attractor-hypothesis.md)

This is the definitive public entry point to the theory. It is maintained at one stable link rather than published as a sequence of competing article versions.

The flagship presents:

- the foundational claim and proposed mechanism;
- Weak IAH and Strong IAH;
- regret, functional distance, and Origin Sensitivity;
- architectural convergence as a central level of application;
- convergence without inheritance;
- evidence requirements and falsification conditions;
- the boundary between the scientific hypothesis and speculative extensions.

**Status:** developing hypothesis; no empirical validation, peer review, institutional affiliation, or academic acceptance is claimed.

## The Two Canonical Forms

**Weak IAH:** functional diversity and origin sensitivity tend to decrease as independently optimized systems approach a shared frontier, while multiple persistent solution families may remain.

**Strong IAH:** at a specified functional level, near-frontier diversity tends to zero and independent systems approach one limiting functional equivalence class.
